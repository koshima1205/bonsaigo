*Ume* (*Prunus mume*) is known in English as Japanese apricot. The Missouri Botanical Garden says it is native to southern China and other parts of East Asia, and is prized for fragrant pink flowers that open in mid to late winter before the leaves emerge. In bonsai it is the flowering tree that announces spring while the cold is still around.

## Flowers before leaves
Ume blooms on bare branches in late winter to early spring. The flowers are white or pink and strongly fragrant. The garden lists the bloom time as February to March in St. Louis, and earlier in mild climates. After the flowers come small fruits that are edible. Timing shifts with the region and the year's weather.

## Prune after flowering
Shape the branches after the flowers finish, in late winter to early spring. Heavy pruning before or during bloom may disrupt flowering, so avoid it. Pinching new shoots in the growing season keeps the tree compact.

## Repot in early spring
Repotting is best in early spring, before new growth starts. A rough guide is every two to three years for young trees, and every three to five years for older ones.

## Sun, water and feeding
Grow it outdoors in full sun; at least five to six hours of direct light a day is suggested. Keep the soil moderately moist with good drainage — more water in the growing season, less in winter. Feed with a balanced solid fertilizer every four to six weeks from spring to early autumn, and rest in winter. A light dose in late winter or early spring is said to encourage earlier blooms.

## About the cold
Ume tolerates some frost but should be protected from prolonged freezing. The botanical garden also warns that cold snaps in late winter can damage flowers and fruit. In colder climates, a sheltered spot or greenhouse is safer.

## Name and fruit
The genus *Prunus* is Latin for plum or cherry tree, and the species name *mume* comes from the Japanese name for the tree. The fruits resemble small apricots about an inch (2.5 cm) across. They are poor eaten fresh but are used for jams and preserves. In bonsai, ume offers both flowers and fruit.

## Summary
- Ume flowers in late winter to early spring, before the leaves; cold can affect it
- Prune after flowering; avoid heavy cuts before or during bloom
- Repot in early spring before buds move; grow outdoors in full sun
