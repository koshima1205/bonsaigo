On a flowering bonsai, the order of work through the year decides the flowers, not just the week they open. Satsuki (Japanese azalea) and ume (Japanese apricot) show the pattern well. Months are a guide for the Kanto plain in Japan; elsewhere, climate shifts them.

## The core pattern: prune right after flowering, buds form in summer, bloom next spring
Satsuki's flower buds for next year form in summer, after flowering ends. That is why you prune as soon as flowers fade. Prune late, and you may get few or no flowers the following year.

## The satsuki year
**Late spring to early summer (bloom and after)** Protect from rain and hot sun while in flower so blooms last. When the show ends, remove spent flowers promptly. One Japanese guide feeds lightly and rests the tree about ten days. Prune from the end of flowering to around June 20, keeping two shoots of average vigor from the many that sprout and cutting to one or two nodes or to the base. Avoid repotting at the same time.

**Summer to autumn** Buds are developing. The English guide advises against heavy pruning after mid-summer. In autumn, only thin buds and lightly wire small branches.

**Winter to early spring** Repotting suits February to March. Satsuki prefers a lime-free, slightly acidic mix, and kanuma is named as a good choice. Rainwater, or rainwater mixed with tap water, is recommended; hard water is unsuitable. Protect from temperatures below about -5 °C (23 °F).

## The ume year
Ume blooms in late winter to early spring, often before its leaves. Prune after flowering, not before or during bloom. Through the growing season, pinch back new shoots.

Repot in early spring before growth starts, every two to three years, or three to five for older trees. Feed from spring to early autumn and stop in winter dormancy. The tree tolerates frost but not prolonged freezing.

For a New Year display, a Japanese guide moves ume indoors from January to early February to a cool entrance and mists the buds.

## Side by side
| | Satsuki | Ume |
|---|---|---|
| Blooms | Late spring | Late winter to early spring |
| Pruning | Right after flowers, by about June 20 | After flowers |
| Repotting | About Feb to Mar | Early spring, before growth |
| Water and soil | Lime-free soil, avoid hard water | Avoid waterlogging |

## Feeding and water
For satsuki, an azalea-type fertilizer is used in the growing season, reduced or stopped while in flower; weekly liquid feed or longer-interval solid granules are both described. Keep the soil moist but not waterlogged, and dunk a root ball that has dried out. For ume, a balanced solid fertilizer every four to six weeks from spring to early autumn is a common guide.

## Switching gears after the bloom
When flowers end, prune, feed lightly and rest follow in quick succession. Remember that the weeks right after blooming decide next year's flowers, and the order stops feeling confusing.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Flowering trees follow prune after bloom, buds in summer, flowers next spring.
- Finish satsuki pruning by about June 20 and avoid heavy pruning after mid-summer.
- Prune ume after bloom and repot in early spring before growth.
