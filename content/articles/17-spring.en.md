Spring is when a bonsai leaves winter dormancy and moves quickly. Repotting, bud work and propagation all fall within a few weeks, so knowing the order helps. Every month named here is a guide for the Kanto plain in Japan (*Kanto heiya*); elsewhere, climate will shift things earlier or later.

## Watch the tree, not the calendar
Bonsai Empire's calendar says plainly that timing follows the tree's own life cycle, and that its months are rough. Local climate, microclimate and latitude all move the dates. Treat a calendar as a starting point and let the swelling buds tell you when to act.

## Repotting (*uekae*) — refreshing roots and soil
Broadleaf trees (*zoki*) are best repotted in the weeks before buds open, at the end of dormancy. Wash the root ball, then trim thick roots so fine feeder roots develop. Even for a vigorous tree, the guide's ceiling for root reduction is about 60 percent.

Conifers keep part of the root ball intact to protect the fungi living with the roots. Their window is wide, from the first half of February to mid-June depending on location. As a Kanto guide, Japanese black pine is repotted from late March to early April, five-needle pine from late March to late April, and maple around March. For two to three weeks afterward, keep the tree in a sunny spot out of the wind. Cut roots take up water slowly, so water lightly and check the soil every day.

## Bud work
On maples, once the first pair of leaves opens, the soft tip between them can be pinched out to keep twigs fine. This weakens the tree over time, so it suits mature trees that already have delicate branching.

Pines split into two groups. "One-flush" pines such as five-needle pine have their candles (new shoots) twisted off by hand, not cut with scissors, in May and June while they are about 2.5 cm long. "Two-flush" pines such as black pine are cut in summer, which the next article (early summer) covers.

## Making more trees: cuttings and air layering
Air layering (*tori-gi*) means coaxing roots out of a trunk or branch so it becomes a new tree; it works best when sap is flowing strongly. Cuttings work for junipers and most broadleaves but are not recommended for pines. A Japanese guide puts zelkova air layering from late May into the rainy season.

## Spring hazards
The main spring risks are frost, drying and wind. When bringing trees out of winter storage, let cold-sensitive species adjust gradually. For black pine, one guide starts solid organic fertilizer in March, repeated about every four weeks. Adjust to the condition of your tree.

## Caring for cuts
When a thick branch is cut in late winter or early spring, smoothing the surface flat helps the wound close neatly. The Kindai Bonsai recipe describes sealing it with a wound paste (*yugozai*) after cleaning, to prevent drying and infection. On pines, a longer stub is sometimes left so resin spreads, to be carved into deadwood (*jin*) later.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Start spring work when buds move, not when the calendar says so.
- Repot around bud swell; after repotting, avoid wind and water lightly.
- Pinching builds fine twigs but weakens the tree, so keep it for refined trees.
