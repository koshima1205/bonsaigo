Japanese white pine (*goyomatsu*, *Pinus parviflora*) gets its Japanese name from the five needles held in each bundle. They are soft and cluster thickly near the ends of the branches. A botanical garden profile says wild trees grow tall, starting out pyramid-shaped and flattening and spreading as they age. Bonsai growers prize the fine foliage and the quiet bearing of the tree.

## Unlike black pine, it grows once
Black pine is cut at *mekiri* to coax out a second flush. White pine extends only one time each year (*ichido-nobi*), so the cut-every-candle routine does not apply. Taking off every candle can leave the tree badly weakened, and it may die.

## Matching candle lengths
New candles lengthen from late spring into early summer. Trim any that race ahead until they match their neighbors, and when three or more rise from a single spot, narrow them to two. The point is to keep vigorous shoots from overpowering the weaker parts. If spring passed without thinning, unneeded shoots can still be removed in autumn.

Candles of similar length let light into the whole canopy and give a well-proportioned outline. Plucking surplus old needles from the strong zones smooths out differences in vigor in much the same way.

## Sunlight and watering
Keep it outside where it gets full sun. The garden profile says soil type matters little provided water drains away, yet the tree likes cool summers and struggles in hot, muggy weather. Too much water harms the roots, so wait for the surface to dry before the next watering. When fresh shoots are growing, try to shield it from hard rain.

## Feeding and repotting
A healthy tree may be fed from spring to autumn. Repot in spring as the buds start to move. Late summer or early autumn is possible too, as long as fine roots have time to form before winter.

## A word on rootstock
Many white pines are grafted onto black pine roots, which helps them grow steadily. When buying, look at the base for the graft union; it shows how the tree was put together.

## Getting to know the tree
The garden profile describes green needles with a slight twist, as long as about 3 inches (7 to 8 cm), with white inner faces. Its oval cones measure two to three inches and can hang on for as long as seven years. Bark on young trees is smooth and grayish black, turning cracked and scaly in time. Admiring that aged surface is part of what draws people to white pine bonsai.

## Summary
- White pine grows once a year; never cut all the candles
- Even out candle lengths and keep at most two shoots per point
- Full sun outdoors, don't overwater, and mind summer heat and humidity
