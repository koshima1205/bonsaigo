Bonsai has a long history that begins in China. Here is the broad outline, focusing on points where the dates are reasonably well documented.

## Landscapes in pots, in China
In China, people had long enjoyed making small mountains and trees on trays and in dishes. By around AD 700, growing dwarfed trees in containers was reportedly under way, and the practice later became known as *penjing* (盆景). Rock arrangements that suggest mountains and cliffs belong to the same family.

## Arriving in Japan
Tray landscapes reached Japan from China at least 1,200 years ago, reportedly as religious souvenirs. Japanese sources trace bonsai back to *bonsan* and potted plants brought over in the Heian period.

Through the Kamakura period and after, Chinese cultural influence helped the habit of growing trees in containers take hold. In the Muromachi period (around the 15th century), a story about potted trees was reportedly adapted into a Noh play.

## Edo-period gardening
In the Edo period, daimyo built gardens and gardening became popular among ordinary townspeople too. By the late 18th century, an annual show of small pines had begun in Kyoto. Around 1800, scholars near Osaka renamed these trees *bonsai*, to set them apart from ordinary potted trees (*hachi-no-ki*).

In the following century, catalogs, books and early formal shows appeared, and wire for shaping branches shifted from hemp to copper and iron.

## Meiji and Omiya
After the Meiji era began, some garden-tree growers turned into bonsai specialists. After the Great Kanto Earthquake of 1923, bonsai growers left Tokyo and resettled in Omiya, in today's Saitama City, where Omiya Bonsai Village grew (see the separate article). After the Second World War, bonsai matured into a respected national art, with apprenticeships, exhibitions and classes for foreign students.

## Out into the world
Japanese dwarf trees reached the West through world fairs and similar events from the late 19th century. One thing that helped fuel overseas popularity was the first World Bonsai Convention, held in Japan in 1989. The eighth convention took place in Saitama City in April 2017, the first in Japan in 28 years.

## Summary (3 lines)
- It starts with Chinese tray landscapes, reaching Japan in the Heian period.
- Edo-period gardening culture led to the name "bonsai" taking hold around 1800.
- Omiya in 1923 and the 1989 world convention carried it to enthusiasts everywhere.
