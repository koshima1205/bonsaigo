Bonsai (盆栽) is the practice of growing a tree or plant in a pot so that it reads as a natural landscape. It is not just a small tree. The aim is to capture, in miniature, the look of an old tree standing on a mountainside or a windy shore.

## What the name means
*Bon* means a shallow tray or dish, and *sai* means a planted tree. Together, the word means roughly "planted in a container."

A common misunderstanding is that bonsai need a special dwarf variety. They do not. Almost any woody species can be used, though trees with small leaves, or leaves that can be made smaller, are easier to shape.

## Careful work that looks effortless
Growing the tree is only the start. Caretakers pinch buds, prune branches and bend them with wire, and they adjust feeding so the tree stays healthy without growing wildly.

The goal is a tree that shows little trace of the human hand. The more work goes in, the more natural it is meant to look, and that quiet paradox is much of the appeal.

## Chinese roots
Bonsai grew out of an older Chinese tradition of making landscapes in containers. That tradition, known as *penjing* (盆景), sometimes includes rocks to suggest mountains and cliffs.

The practice is generally described as reaching Japan from China in the Heian period, then developing its own character there. Today it has enthusiasts in the United States, France, Italy, Spain and many other countries.

## Not finished in one lifetime
A bonsai is never truly finished. It is alive, so it changes a little every year. Some trees are several hundred years old, passed from owner to owner or placed in the hands of specialists for their care.

Over such a long life, a tree may be restyled more than once. The idea that a bonsai can outlast the person who shaped it is one of the art's deepest attractions.

## Sizes and pots
Bonsai range from tiny trees that fit in a palm to large ones that take several people to lift. The pot is usually shallow and made of fired clay, and it works like a frame that sets off the tree.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Bonsai is a living form that recreates a natural landscape in a pot.
- It began in China and grew into a distinct Japanese culture.
- It is never finished in one generation; it is handed on.
