You need no special knowledge to enjoy bonsai. But knowing where to look makes the experience far richer.

## Two broad families
Bonsai trees fall into two broad groups.

*Shōhaku* (松柏) are the evergreen conifers, mainly pines and shimpaku juniper. Japanese five-needle pine and black pine are described as sturdy and relatively easy to grow. *Shimpaku* is a cypress-family evergreen that grows wild on high mountains and seaside cliffs that likes a sunny spot.

*Zōki* (雑木) covers every other tree. It includes foliage trees such as maples, flowering trees, and fruiting trees such as quince (*karin*), crabapples and winterberry (*ume-modoki*). Zōki are also admired for their branch structure after the leaves fall.

## Start at the base
Look first at the base. Roots that spread firmly, gripping the soil, show that the tree has stood there a long time. Roots that fan out like a turtle's shell are called *bankon* (盤根). Roots reaching in all directions make a tree look stable.

## Then the trunk
Next, the trunk. Bark that has gathered years tells of the tree's age. Follow how the trunk rises from the base, and notice its bends and changes in thickness.

## Then the branches
Look at the arrangement of the branches. Well-kept branching strongly shapes the overall impression. Deciduous trees have charm in winter, once the leaves have fallen, so viewing the same tree in different seasons pays off.

## Shari and jin
A part of the trunk that has died back and been bared to white wood is called *shari* (舎利). The same effect on a branch is called *jin* (神). They evoke a tree that has lived through hard years, and are a highlight of bonsai viewing. They are sometimes created by hand as well.

## From every side
Bonsai are viewed from every direction while imagining a grand natural scene. Do not stop at the front: look at the sides and back too. At the front the trunk is shown to advantage, while from behind foliage adds depth.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Think of trees as shōhaku or zōki (foliage, flowering and fruiting trees).
- Move from roots to trunk to branches, and the tree's history appears.
- Shari and jin speak of time and the forces of nature.
