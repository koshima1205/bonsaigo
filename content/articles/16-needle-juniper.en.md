Needle juniper (*nezu*, *Juniperus rigida*) is an evergreen of the cypress family, also called temple juniper. A Hokkaido University plant database lists it as *nezu* (also *nezumisashi*), found in northern China, Korea and Japan as far north as the Tohoku region, growing on rocky, sunny sites and tolerant of sea salt and drought. Its uses are given as garden tree, hedge and bonsai.

## Not the same as shimpaku
The names are easy to mix up, but shimpaku belongs to the *Juniperus chinensis* group (the cliff-dwelling *miyama-byakushin* type), while *nezu* is a different species, *Juniperus rigida*. The most obvious difference is the foliage. Needle juniper has sharp, dark green, stinging needles with a narrow white line along each one; Chinese juniper has scale-like foliage. Because its foliage is rigid, branches are often less flexible and need more careful wiring.

## Outdoors, in sun
Keep it outdoors all year in a bright spot; it cannot live indoors. Below about -10 °C (15 °F), protect it. Some junipers turn purplish-brown in frost and return to green in spring. The roots dislike excess moisture, so let the soil dry slightly between waterings. Repot about every two years in early spring, a little later than Chinese juniper, once new growth starts, and avoid cutting roots hard.

## How to handle it
Don't shear it like a hedge; use sharp scissors on shoots that extend beyond the silhouette. Removing every tip weakens the tree. As with shimpaku, bare branches do not bud again, so keep green on any branch you want to keep. Before wiring, wrap branches with raffia or tape, and take care with deadwood, which breaks easily. Thin the foliage pads so light and air reach the interior.

## Rust: dispose of infected branches
Junipers can get rust, a fungal disease. Once present it cannot be cured, and visibly infested branches usually die. Removing the galls does not guarantee the fungus won't return. The disease cycles between junipers and trees such as pear and hawthorn. Do not compost infected material; dispose of it. For any chemical treatment, ask a specialist. The source also notes that Japanese junipers rarely get rust, and blue-green varieties resist it better than yellowish-green ones.

## Summary
- Needle juniper is a separate species from shimpaku, with sharp needles and a white line
- Grow outdoors in sun; water after the soil dries slightly
- Dispose of rust-infected branches; don't compost them, and ask a specialist about treatment
