Every month here is a guide for the Kanto plain in Japan (*Kanto heiya*). Overseas, in colder or warmer regions, expect shifts. The Japanese source groups work in two-month blocks such as "January to February," so months overlap. Use this as a map of the flow, not a rulebook.

## January to February (dormancy, winter)
- Winter protection: shield pots from freezing and wind
- Pruning and restyling of plum and conifers
- Wiring, with attention to species
- Deadwood work (*jin* and *shari*, the pale bare wood) also appears in this period
- Satsuki azalea repotting is suited to February to March

## March to April (waking)
- Bring trees out of winter storage, cold-sensitive ones gradually
- Repot broadleaves and conifers; for black pine, late March to early April
- Air layering, cuttings, grafting, sowing seed
- After repotting, avoid wind and water lightly

## April to May (new growth)
- Bud pinching and leaf work; on maples, pinch as buds open
- Fertilizing begins
- Five-needle pine candles are evened out in May to June

## May to June (after flowers)
- Prune satsuki as soon as flowers fade, by about June 20
- Black pine candle cutting starts around June 20
- Rainy-season watering and checking for wire bite

## June to July (early summer)
- Black pine candle cutting, finishing by early July
- Defoliation of broadleaves, air layering
- Reduce top-dressed fertilizer during the rains

## July to August (midsummer)
- Water two to three times a day, but go easy on stressed trees
- Afternoon shade and placement tricks
- Avoid repotting conifers

## September to October (autumn)
- Resume fertilizing once below about 30 °C (late August to early September)
- Black pine needle thinning by October, red pine by early October
- Wiring, and old-needle removal on five-needle pine

## November to December (leaf fall, winter prep)
- Prune broadleaves right after leaf fall
- Enjoy maple color first
- From late December, by a central-Japan guide, move vulnerable trees into storage after a cold spell or two

## The limits of a calendar, and what craftsperson Q&A can add
Regional differences are large; even within Kanto, city and hillside trees wake at different times. Bonsai Empire likewise says timing shifts with temperature, placement and latitude, and urges watching the tree's growth cycle over a calendar.

A monthly list tells you what usually happens, not when to touch this tree this year. We plan to fill that gap with craftsperson answers (Q&A).

## How to use this list
First, decide whether your tree is a conifer, a broadleaf or a flowering tree, and read that season's entries. Then check whether buds and temperatures are running ahead of or behind a normal year, and shift the work by a few weeks. The Japanese source gives species-specific timing in detail, so read this list alongside the species articles (black pine, five-needle pine, shimpaku, maple, satsuki, ume) and the seasonal articles.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Months are a Kanto plain guide; climate shifts them.
- The year runs dormancy, waking, bud work, heat care, then tidying in autumn.
- In the end, trust the tree over the calendar.
