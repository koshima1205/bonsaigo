Satsuki is an azalea (*Rhododendron indicum*). The Chicago Botanic Garden notes it blooms in May to June; the straight species is red, while cultivars vary widely. In bonsai it is a leading example of a flowering tree. What matters most is when you prune, and what soil and water you use.

## Prune right after flowering
The key rule: prune as soon as the flowers finish. Remove the faded flowers and the seed-forming bases, and shape the branches at the same time. The reason is that next year's flower buds form in summer, after flowering. Prune too late, and next year's flowers may be few or almost none. Heavy pruning later in the season can cut off the buds themselves.

Satsuki tolerates strong pruning and can sprout from bare branches. Unwanted shoots at the base of the trunk or branches can be removed at any time of year. The wood is brittle, though, so wire and bend with care.

## Soil and water
Azaleas like slightly acidic, lime-free soil; pure *kanuma* (a Japanese bonsai soil) is cited as a good example. Hard tap water is unsuitable. Rainwater, a rain-and-tap mix, or filtered water is better.

## Protecting the bloom
While the tree is in flower, shelter it from rain and hot sun so the blooms last longer. Cool temperatures and steady moisture help too. The Chicago garden likewise says bright light without direct sun prolongs bloom. Pause feeding or halve the dose during flowering.

## Repotting and feeding
The roots are fine and matted, so repot about every two years, in spring or after flowering, and prune roots carefully. During the growing season, feed with an azalea fertilizer.

## Remove the seed bases, keep the shoots
After flowering, remove not just the faded flowers but also the seed-forming bases (ovaries), so the tree puts its energy into new shoots and next year's buds rather than seed.

In the growing season, feed with a liquid fertilizer weekly, or spread organic granules on the surface at longer intervals.

## Summary
- Prune right after flowering; flower buds form in the summer that follows
- Use acidic, lime-free soil (kanuma) and soft water, not hard tap water
- Shelter from rain and strong sun during bloom; pause or halve feeding
