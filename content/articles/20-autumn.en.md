Autumn is when you steady trees tired by summer and prepare them for winter and next year. Months are a guide for the Kanto plain in Japan; climate elsewhere shifts them.

## Start feeding again
Once temperatures fall below about 30 °C (86 °F), fertilizing resumes, typically late August to early September on the Kanto plain. For autumn the guide suggests less nitrogen and more phosphorus, for roots and spring flowers, plus potassium for strength. Organic fertilizers need roughly 20 to 30 days before roots can use them, so plan early. A Japanese guide keeps feeding maple and quince through October.

## Needle thinning and old needles
Needle thinning (*hasukashi*) means removing crowded needles to let light and air in. Kindai Bonsai says black pine thinning should be done by October and postponed to spring if missed; red pine by early October.

On pines such as five-needle pine, old needles are plucked from vigorous areas in autumn to even out strength. One guide removes last year's old needles in October.

## Wiring
An English pine guide places wiring from early autumn to early spring. In Japanese guidance, red pine is finished by early October, while shimpaku juniper and five-needle pine can be wired now. Broadleaves keep their wire through to the next spring. Satsuki gets only light wire on small branches. As branches thicken, wire can bite, so check from time to time.

## Fall color and light
For maples, enjoy the color first, then strip the leaves and prune. One Japanese guide says mountain maple colors better if left outdoors to the morning and evening chill. Keep trees outside rather than in a warm room.

Deciduous trees are pruned right after leaf fall. Sap starts to bleed from cuts roughly 1 to 10 days after the leaves are gone, so the window is short.

## Before winter
Deeply pruned or wired trees go into a storage space (*muro*) for winter to keep them from freezing. Trees with fruit need attention to drying and birds.

## Autumn watering and the logic of feeding
Soil dries less in autumn than in summer, so watering gets careless. Kindai Bonsai lists Ezo spruce, *mayumi* and fruiting trees as ones that still need watching for dryness. Chemical fertilizers act quickly while organic ones take time, so knowing the difference helps you avoid overfeeding before winter. Each tree also has its own stopping point; Ezo spruce, for example, is not fed after the end of October.

## Trees repotted in autumn
Most trees are repotted in spring, but pyracantha and crabapple are examples repotted in autumn. The English pine guide says one-flush pines can also be repotted from late summer to early autumn if enough time remains for fine roots to regrow before winter. All of this depends on the species and region, so when unsure, spring is the safer choice.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Resume feeding once below 30 °C; late August to early September on the Kanto plain.
- Do needle thinning and wiring by about October; red pine by early October.
- Enjoy maple color first, then prune; prune deciduous trees right after leaf fall.
