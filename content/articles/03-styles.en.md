A style (*jukei* 樹形) is the overall shape of the tree: whether the trunk is straight, curving or leaning. The same species can look like an entirely different landscape depending on the style. Here are the main ones you will meet.

## Standing upright
*Chokkan* (直幹, formal upright) has a straight trunk that tapers from a thick base to a slender top. Branching begins fairly low on the trunk, and a single branch forms the apex.

*Moyogi* (模様木, informal upright) has a trunk that winds in an S-shape, with a branch at each bend. The taper is still important. It is a natural-looking, approachable style.

*Hokidachi* (箒立ち, broom) has a straight trunk with fine branches fanning out above to form a rounded crown. It suits deciduous trees with fine twigs.

## Leaning and hanging
*Shakan* (斜幹, slanting) leans at roughly 60 to 80 degrees. Roots grow strongest on the side opposite the lean, and the first branch is placed on that side to balance the tree.

*Kengai* (懸崖, cascade) rises briefly, then bends sharply down past the pot. It recalls a tree hanging from a cliff and is usually planted in a tall pot. In *han-kengai* (半懸崖, semi-cascade) the trunk does not fall below the bottom of the pot.

*Fukinagashi* (吹き流し, windswept) has trunk and branches all streaming one way, as if blown by constant wind.

## Slender and sparse
*Bunjingi* (文人木, literati) has a thin, winding trunk with almost no branches until the top, where the foliage gathers. It is sometimes set in a small round pot, and has a quiet, spare character.

## Many trunks
Two trunks from one root system are *sokan* (双幹); three or more are *kabudachi* (株立ち). Several trees in one pot, arranged as a small woodland, are *yose-ue* (寄せ植え).

## Choosing a front
A bonsai has a *front*: the side where the tree looks best and the movement of the trunk is easiest to read. The Japanese agriculture ministry's article describes the front as the face that shows the trunk and sets off the tree's form, the sides as angled slightly toward the viewer, and the back as the face where foliage adds depth. Bonsai are meant to be enjoyed from every direction, but choosing a front gives the tree its leading role. How to choose differs from artisan to artisan, so this article only opens the door.

## Summary (3 lines)
- A style is the overall shape set by the trunk's curve and lean.
- Chokkan, moyogi, shakan, kengai and bunjingi are the best-known examples.
- The front is the tree's best face; learn how to choose it from the artisans.
