Here are the four basic jobs that shape a bonsai and keep it healthy. Timing shifts with climate and species, so watch the tree itself.

## Two kinds of pruning
**Maintenance pruning** keeps the shape: you trim shoots that have grown past the intended outline, which pushes growth inward. For outdoor trees, it is done through the growing season, roughly early spring to late autumn, about two to four times a year.

**Structural pruning** sets the tree's basic form by removing larger branches. Cuts cannot be undone, so plan first. It is generally done in early spring, sometimes in late autumn. Remove dead branches first, then decide which to keep. Large wounds are sometimes sealed with cut paste. A healthy tree can generally lose about one-third of its foliage at most, and it is wise not to repot or cut roots in the same season.

## Pinch pines by hand
Cutting pines and some other conifers with shears can leave brown, dead tips, so they are pinched by hand. Hold the tip of the shoot between thumb and forefinger and pull it away so it snaps at its weakest point. This is *memitsumi* (芽摘み), bud pinching.

## Wiring
Wire bends branches into shape. Anodized aluminum is easier to handle, suits deciduous trees, and is where beginners usually start. Annealed copper is harder and is used on pines and conifers. A common guide is wire about one-third the thickness of the branch.

Wrap at about 45 degrees, working from base to tip, and anchor the start with one or two turns around the trunk. To bend a branch down, bring the wire from below; to bend it up, from above. Wire every intended branch before bending any, and bend from the inside of the curve to reduce the risk of splitting.

As branches thicken, wire can bite in and leave scars. Branches often set within a few months, but growth is fast in the growing season, so check often and remove the wire before it cuts in. Cut it off turn by turn rather than unwinding it, which can damage the tree.

## Repotting
Repot in early spring, while the tree is dormant and buds are just starting to swell. Avoid summer and winter; some species can be repotted in autumn. It is not a yearly routine: check the roots and repot if they circle the root mass. A rough guide is about every 2 years for fast growers and every 3 to 5 years for mature trees.

The steps: cut the anchoring wires, lift out the tree, work out old soil gently with a chopstick, trim overlong roots, replant in fresh soil, and water thoroughly. Remove no more than 30% of the roots. For pines, leave at least half the root mass untouched to protect beneficial fungi.

After repotting, a tree may look briefly weak. Keep its position stable and its moisture steady, and shelter it from extremes of heat, cold, sun, drying and wind. Avoid heavy pruning and early feeding. New growth usually appears within a few weeks, though full recovery can take a season.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Think of pruning as two jobs: keeping shape and building structure.
- Wire at about 45 degrees and remove it before it bites in.
- Repot in early spring, guided by the roots.
