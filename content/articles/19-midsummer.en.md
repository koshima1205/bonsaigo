Midsummer care is less about new work than about protecting the tree from heat. Months given are a guide for the Kanto plain in Japan; elsewhere, climate will shift them.

## Watering by the tree, not the clock
Once the rainy season ends, soil dries much faster. A Japanese guide says three waterings a day if you can, and morning plus evening if you cannot. Bonsai Empire advises heavy watering when temperatures stay above 32 to 35 °C (90 to 95 °F), with misting if needed, because without water leaves and then branches can die.

A tree already stressed by heat, however, has weakened roots. Heavy watering then keeps the soil wet and can make things worse. If leaf tips begin to brown, move the tree to half shade, tilt the pot so excess water drains, and mist the leaves while it recovers.

## Leaf scorch — leaves browning from strong sun and heat
Maple scorch comes from strong direct sun combined with heat, wind or dry roots. Once it passes about 29 to 30 °C (85 °F), the advice is to move maples to partial shade during the hottest part of the day. A Japanese guide suggests blocking the sun from around 2 p.m.

For shade, use shade cloth (*kanreisha*); fences, shelves or reed screens (*sudare*) also work. Protect the heat-sensitive first: maples, satsuki azaleas and hydrangeas.

## Placement tricks
- Group sun-loving trees that dry fast, so watering is easier.
- For small bonsai, sink the pot halfway into gravel in a shallow tray, or put it inside a larger pot, to keep the pot cooler. Covering the soil surface also helps.
- Give five-needle pine wide spacing to avoid stuffiness; misting in the evening is also suggested.

## Work to postpone
Sources advise against repotting conifers in summer. Summer repotting of broadleaves is described only for June, after leaves harden, not for peak heat. Heavy pruning is better saved for cooler weather, but note that the sources spell this out for repotting, and pruning depends on the species. When unsure about a particular tree, ask a craftsperson.

Fertilizing resumes once temperatures drop below about 30 °C, so most growers go light during the hottest weeks.

## Trees that must not run dry
Kindai Bonsai names Ezo spruce, cedar, cypress, fruiting pyracantha, *mayumi* and gardenia, among others, as trees needing close watching. Fruiting trees need extra care while fruit is on.

## Summary (3 lines)
- Never let trees dry out in heat, but do not overwater a stressed one.
- Prevent scorch with afternoon shade and steady water.
- Leave big jobs, such as conifer repotting, until it cools.
